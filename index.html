Operator Briefing

How to play

You run a city's power grid in real time. A 24-hour clock ticks; demand rises and falls with the daily curve and climbs every day you survive. Keep capacity ≥ demand.

The loop

  1. Baseload & renewables (nuclear, coal, solar, wind) run automatically. Solar and wind vary with time of day and weather.
  2. Peakers (gas) and the battery are your reserves — arm them as the peak approaches. 123 arm peakers, B fires the battery, or tap a fleet row. They take time to spin up, so pre-arm them.
  3. Manage grid pauses the clock to build capacity, upgrade plants (Mk tiers), and service worn ones.
  4. Hit Space to pause anytime.

When it goes wrong

BrownoutA small shortfall — lost sales, fines, falling satisfaction, and a short window to recover.
BlackoutA big or sustained shortfall — the run ends.
Lost confidenceLet satisfaction fall to 0 (chronic brownouts) and the city pulls your licence.

Field questions

Why did a plant trip?
Trips are rare and reserved for neglect. A plant at 85% or better NEVER trips — keep your fleet healthy and you'll almost never see one. Risk only appears below 85% and climbs as it wears: ~1%/day at 70%, ~6% at 60%, ~18% at 50%. Service before 50%: below that a plant needs a full Overhaul — offline longer, at a steep premium. One about to go first shows a ⚠ STRAINING warning for a few seconds — arm reserves, shed load, or stand a straining peaker down on your terms.
Why does my reserve margin keep swinging?
The grid is live and volatile — solar clouds over and clears, wind gusts and lulls, and demand wobbles with the occasional short spike (a cold front, an industrial swing). Following that moving net load is the real job: keep a cushion of armable reserves and fire a peaker or the battery when the margin dips. A brief shortfall fills the strain meter (seconds to react), so attention beats panic.
What wears a plant out?
How hard you run it, and how old it is. A plant leaned on near full load on a tight grid deteriorates far faster than a lightly-loaded reserve, and every plant wears quicker as it ages — so your round-the-clock workhorses need servicing most. A service restores condition and takes some age off the clock; an upgrade (Mk tier) is a deeper refit that takes off more — but age never fully resets, so late in a run old iron is worth replacing.
How do I earn?
You sell served power two ways: contracted blocks settle at a price you locked in at the Desk, and the rest clears at the live spot price. Spot rises when your grid is tight and crashes when you're flush — so thin-but-covered is lucrative, but a shortfall is never a windfall (unserved load earns nothing). Hedge with contracts to tame the swings. The wholesale price up top is a merit-order signal of what it costs to run plants right now (carbon lifts it for dirty plants).
What's the carbon price?
From day 3 it rises, adding cost to fossil plants and eventually making gas cheaper to run than coal.
Why is my cash falling when nothing's wrong?
Thermal plants (nuclear, coal, gas) have a fixed operating cost each hour, running or not — idle capacity bleeds money, so retire what you don't need in Manage for salvage. Solar and wind are cheap to own.
What carries between runs?
Meta-currency (⚡) earned at the end of a run buys permanent Skills. Everything else resets.
