How the economics work

Each year is simulated over a representative 24-hour day and scaled to 365 days. Power is in MW, energy in MWh (MW×h), prices in $/MWh.

Dispatch follows the merit order: cheapest resources run first — free wind & solar, then stored battery energy, then thermal plants ranked by marginal cost. Batteries charge from midday renewable surplus and discharge into the evening peak.

Price each hour is the marginal cost of the last plant dispatched (uniform-price auction). You earn that price on every MWh you serve, so cheap plants earn "inframarginal rent". A carbon price adds $/t × tCO₂/MWh to each fossil plant's cost — enough of it flips coal behind gas, then behind clean energy.

Lose if cash falls below −$150M or a year's reliability drops under 85%. Win by running the grid to 2050 solvent — graded on profit, reliability and emissions.
